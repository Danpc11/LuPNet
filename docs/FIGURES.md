# Figures of the article

Built by `src/make_figures_story.py --clinical FILE` (Figures 1–6; the panels with patients need the clinical file, and Figures 5 and 6 the tables of `noninvasive_tool.py --clinical FILE`). Simulated cases are runs of the model; patients are 47 adults with fibrosing interstitial lung disease and right heart catheterisation (43 with FVC, DLCO and mPAP).

Layout and colour. Every figure is a fixed grid of cells on a 180 mm canvas; the panel letter sits in the top-left corner of its cell, so letters are aligned and those of the first column are flush with the left edge. One colour-blind-safe palette (Okabe–Ito) is used throughout and a colour always means the same thing: grey, no lesion or the calibrated pattern; vermilion, a vascular lesion and the diffuse lesion; amber, a lesion spreading from the scar; pink, the mixed lesion; blue, the UIP pattern and the estimate of the model for a patient; green, the NSIP pattern; black, measurements in patients; tissue (preserved, fibrotic, honeycomb) in greys.

**Figure 1. One shear-stress rule builds the normal human pulmonary circulation.** A) The model: arterial tree, gas-exchange units (preserved, fibrotic, honeycomb), venous tree, and the vascular lesion in series with the arteriole of a unit. B) Diameter by Strahler order of the human pulmonary arterial tree (circles), of the shared law fitted with one free scale and exponent b = 0.9 (solid) and with the systemic exponent (dashed). C) Predicted against measured diameter for arteries (fitted) and veins (not refitted); the factor is the geometric mean error. D) Diameter error against the exponent b, with pulsatile sensing and with mean shear only; dotted lines, the systemic exponent and Murray's law. E) mPAP of the healthy model lung with the per-vessel law and with the shared law; grey band, healthy adults (mean ± SD). F) Diameter by generation of the explicit tree of the model (scale fixed on the main pulmonary artery, b = 0.925) and the measured value at matched flow. G) Pressure drop per Strahler order of the arterial tree.

**Figure 2. Fibrosis alone does not produce pulmonary hypertension, and it multiplies the effect of a lesion.** A) Two ways to raise pulmonary artery pressure in the model: loss of gas-exchange units, which moves the flow to the remaining ones, and a lesion that narrows the arterioles. Channels, gas-exchange units between the pulmonary artery and the veins; dark units, scar; line width, blood flow. B) mPAP against the fraction of lung lost in 60 simulated cases without lesion (grey, one line per case) and the fitted pressure surface (black). C) mPAP against FVC at the visit of the same 60 cases, with the linear fit. D) mPAP against FVC in 47 catheterised patients, with the linear fit. E) mPAP against lung lost for lesions of severity 0 to 8 (surface fitted to 11,022 simulated visits, R² = 0.998). F) mPAP added by a lesion of severity 2 and 4 at 5%, 30% and 60% of lung lost. Dotted lines, 25 mmHg. Lesion severity is the resistance added to each terminal arteriole, in units of its normal resistance.

**Figure 3. The lesion that raises pressure sits in preserved lung.** A) Four locations of the same lesion: scar only; spreading from the scar; mixed (0.5 of the severity in every unit plus 1.5 times the severity spreading); diffuse. Channels, gas-exchange units; dark units, scar; orange marks, lesion on the arteriole (lighter, half severity); line width, blood flow. B) The four locations in one simulated lung with 40% of its units lost: grey, scar; colour scale, lesion in each preserved unit. C) Median mPAP of the simulated cases carrying a lesion, by lung lost, for each location (same fibrosis and same lesion draws in the four cohorts; 140 cases each). D) Correlation between mPAP and FVC in mild-to-moderate disease (FVC 50–90%; n = 113–114). E) Cases with mPAP > 40 mmHg among those with FVC < 50% (n = 144–147; no advanced cohort for the scar-only lesion). F) Cases with mPAP ≥ 25 mmHg among those with FVC ≥ 70% (n = 22 per cohort), and in the patients. Bars, estimate; error bars, 95% bootstrap interval; dashed lines, patients with IPF from the literature. Colours of C–F as the titles of A.

**Figure 4. The result holds for the UIP and the fibrotic NSIP pattern.** A) One simulated lung of each pattern with 40% of its units lost. B) Pressure categories in mild-to-moderate disease (FVC 50–90%) with the calibrated, UIP and NSIP patterns, and in patients with IPF (diamonds). C) Cases with mPAP ≥ 25 mmHg among those with FVC < 50%; dashed line, transplant candidates with IPF. D) mPAP against lung lost for lesions of severity 0, 2 and 4 with the UIP (solid) and NSIP (dashed) pattern. E) DLCO against FVC at the visit, with linear fits. F) Years from the healthy lung to the visit, one point per simulated case; bar, median. Error bars, 95% bootstrap interval.

**Figure 5. In catheterised patients, pressure does not follow lung function.** A, B) mPAP against FVC and DLCO by tomographic pattern, with the linear fit of all patients. C) Patients with mPAP ≥ 25 mmHg by FVC, and the simulated cases with a lesion spreading from the scar and with a diffuse lesion. D) mPAP of each patient measured by catheterisation (circles) and the split of the model estimate into the aged lung, the lung lost and the vascular lesion (bars); patients ranked by measured mPAP. Dotted lines, 25 mmHg.

**Figure 6. Flow unmasks the lesion, and it can be identified without catheterisation.** A) mPAP at rest and at imposed cardiac outputs of 7.5 and 10 L/min in the 55 simulated cases with resting mPAP < 25 mmHg, one line per case; dotted line, 25 mmHg. B) Cases reaching 25 mmHg at each flow; error bars, 95% bootstrap interval. C) The estimate for a patient: FVC and DLCO, and any of the optional measurements, select the simulated visits compatible with them; their distribution gives the probability of PH, mPAP with its interval, and the parts due to the lung lost and to the lesion. D) Area under the ROC curve for mPAP ≥ 25 mmHg in 43 patients, cross-validated, for the model-based estimate (range over repeats) and for logistic regression on the same inputs in the patients with complete data. E) Estimated probability of PH of each patient by catheterisation result, with FVC and DLCO and with every optional input; dotted lines, 0.3 and 0.7. F) Estimated against measured mPAP with every optional input, with the linear fit; bars, 90% interval; dotted line, identity.
